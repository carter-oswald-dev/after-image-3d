TipsDim room, screen brightness up, hold your eyes still on the dot. High-contrast, colourful pictures work best.

Head tracking: the image shifts with your head so the dot stays straight ahead of your eyes, a bit like the New 3DS face tracking. Your starting position counts as “centre”; press C during a run to re-centre. Tune “Follow strength” until the dot seems to stay put in front of you. The camera needs HTTPS or localhost (GitHub Pages is fine).

Two frames, one per eye. After staring, open both eyes and wink left · right · left · right — the afterimage flips between the frames.

Left = frame A (left eye), right = frame B (right eye). Tap to move the dot — it stays in the same spot on both. Photos and SVGs both work.

TipsInterleaved: the image swaps when it beeps — move your hand to cover the other eye. Keep looking at the same dot position. Two similar frames (a small change between them) read best as motion. When dropping two files at once they're sorted by name, so frame1 / frame2 land in order.

A stereo pair, one image per eye. After staring, open both eyes on the blank screen and the afterimages fuse into depth.

Left = left-eye view, right = right-eye view. Tap to move the dot. Photos and SVGs both work.

TipsMost stereo photos online are parallel (L | R). Cross-view photos are R | L — tick “Cross-eyed layout” for those. If the depth looks inside-out, hit Swap L/R. For a phone in a Cardboard-style viewer, pick “parallel” and set panel width to ~48 vw in landscape.
